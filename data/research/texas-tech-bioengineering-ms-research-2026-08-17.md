# Texas Tech University M.S. in Bioengineering — Working Official-Source Notes

**Review date:** 2026-08-17  
**Status:** Distinct in-scope degree structure confirmed; admissions, funding, waiver, and image reconciliation pending.

## Degree identity and scope

The current Texas Tech University Whitacre College of Engineering Bioengineering pages establish an interdisciplinary **M.S. in Bioengineering**. The degree is directly within the agreed biomedical-engineering scope: the official description identifies training for biomedical engineering research and development and lists bioinstrumentation, bioinformatics, biomaterials, biomechanics, biochemistry, biomedical imaging, and occupational bio-ergonomics. It explicitly links its graduate outcomes to medical-device, pharmaceutical, hospital, and government opportunities.

## Current structure

The program offers 30-credit thesis, non-thesis, and non-thesis online pathways. Accepted students are initially considered non-thesis and may switch to a thesis option after securing a suitable thesis advisor. The program page states mathematics through differential equations plus one semester each of chemistry and calculus-based physics as background expectations. The current degree page was updated July 10, 2026.

## Graduate School reconciliation

The current Graduate School application page directs applicants to the Texas Tech graduate application and says program deadlines vary. Its operating policy gives Graduate School priority processing dates for domestic applicants of June 1 (Fall), September 1 (Spring), and March 1 (Summer); its international-admissions page gives recommended January 15 (Fall/Summer) and June 15 (Spring) dates. Both sources warn departments may impose stricter requirements, so these will be presented as Graduate School priority guidance rather than program final deadlines.

The current international Graduate School page accepts DET 100, TOEFL iBT 79, IELTS Academic 6.5, PTE 60, or specified Cambridge/ELS alternatives, with a two-year score-validity window. The Graduate School policy says academic programs may require standardized tests, but the reviewed Bioengineering M.S. sources publish no current program-specific GRE requirement; the profile will leave GRE blank rather than infer one.

The Graduate School policy documents fee-waiver eligibility for qualifying TTU System personnel and their eligible families, McNair/GEM scholars, and active-duty or veteran U.S. military personnel; the international page also documents the employee waiver route. The reviewed current sources do not safely establish a common M.S. base fee across applicant types, so the fee field will remain blank. Graduate School fellowship language is limited and says most new-student assistance is departmental; no BME M.S.-specific funding guarantee was found.
