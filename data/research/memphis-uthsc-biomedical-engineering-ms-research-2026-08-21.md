# University of Memphis–UTHSC Joint M.S. in Biomedical Engineering — Working Official-Source Notes

**Review date:** 2026-08-21  
**Status:** Distinct in-scope master’s degree confirmed; fee, waiver, current image, and final publication decision pending.

## Degree identity and scope

The current UTHSC College of Graduate Health Sciences page establishes that the University of Memphis/UTHSC Joint Graduate Program awards an **M.S. in Biomedical Engineering** with thesis or project options alongside the existing Ph.D. The current University of Memphis BME page describes the M.S. as thesis and non-thesis, confirms degree awards from both institutions, and identifies research in biomaterials/regenerative medicine, biomechanics/research and biomedical devices, neuro/cardiac electrophysiology, MRI/ultrasound imaging, and chemical/bio-sensors and diagnostics.

## Admissions, application, and deadlines

The current University of Memphis BME page directs Fall applicants to the UTHSC-hosted Liaison portal, states that applications are shared across both institutions, lists transcripts, three recommendations, a personal statement, résumé, and international WES/English evidence, and gives **May 15** as the Fall deadline. It says Spring/Summer applications are case-by-case. The page accepts TOEFL, IELTS, PTE, or Duolingo evidence for international applicants, with waiver consideration on a case-by-case basis.

The current UTHSC program page presents a 3.0 bachelor’s GPA, combined GRE 300 (verbal plus quantitative), Internet TOEFL 79/evidence of proficiency, three letters, and May 15 deadline; it warns the program may apply higher cutoffs especially for doctoral applicants. The M.S. profile should not generalize that higher-cutoff warning beyond its relevant source boundary.

## Funding

The official UTHSC application page states that all applicants are considered for any aid available at application time, while University of Memphis notes financial aid may be separate from admissions and recommends applying early. This permits only a qualified “available” funding status, not a guarantee.
