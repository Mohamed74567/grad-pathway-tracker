## Georgia Tech–Emory Biomedical Engineering refresh and publication

Georgia Tech and Emory jointly confer the full-time Biomedical Engineering Ph.D. through Coulter BME. The official catalog and department page establish its life-science, engineering, mathematics, ethics, teaching, minor, advisor-matching, and dissertation structure. The department application page provides a December 1 joint-doctoral deadline that is retained as annual guidance rather than a dated planner entry. Georgia Tech Graduate Education establishes the US$105 international fee, TOEFL/IELTS treatment, and no-Duolingo policy; the reviewed source does not provide a domestic fee. BME official pages describe financial-aid packages and opportunities but do not provide a universal guarantee, so doctoral funding is qualified as available.

The newly published M.S. in Biomedical Engineering has thesis and non-thesis tracks, each totaling 30 credits, with research, clinical immersion, industry collaboration, and focus areas including biomechanics, imaging, biomaterials, and neuroengineering. The official M.S. handbook says thesis projects do not normally include stipend or tuition reimbursement, so departmental funding is marked not applicable. Both profiles use credited official Coulter BME research imagery.

## Georgia Tech interdisciplinary Bioengineering publication

Georgia Tech’s Bioengineering Ph.D. and M.S. are interdisciplinary programs administered through one of eight home schools, preserving students’ access to a broad participating-faculty base. ## University of Georgia Biomedical Engineering Ph.D. fee-waiver refresh — August 27, 2026

The current UGA Graduate School application-fee page confirms US$75 for the first application and US$25 for each additional application. A limited number of fee waivers are available only to applicants who participated in qualifying programs; applicants must upload supporting documentation within the application. The request is one-time and applies to one admissions cycle. Selecting a waiver without eligibility can cause the application to be cancelled, and the fee is non-refundable; an application without payment or an approved waiver is not processed. UGA College of Engineering directs applicants to the Graduate School policy, and the reviewed Biomedical Engineering Ph.D. sources publish no program-specific code or automatic waiver. The guidance was refreshed on the Ph.D. profile only; no policy was transferred to a separate M.S. or Biological Engineering profile.
