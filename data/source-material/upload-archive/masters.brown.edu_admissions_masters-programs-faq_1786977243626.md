## Considering a Brown Master’s?

Brown master's tuition varies by program. Many Brown master's programs require between eight to twelve courses and total tuition averages $77,000 or $38,500 a year, before applicable fees and living expenses (excluding MFA programs). Please note, many programs also offer merit scholarships, and some offer paid co-ops and internships to help offset costs.

The best way to understand the cost is to [review the tuition and fee information](https://sfs.brown.edu/tuition-and-fees/graduate) for the specific program you're considering.

Yes. Many Brown master's programs offer merit-based scholarships, although availability, eligibility, and award amounts vary by program.

Some programs offer scholarships of up to 50% of tuition. Applicants should review the funding information for their specific program for details.

Yes. Several Brown master's programs offer merit-based scholarships that are not based on financial need.

Scholarship decisions and criteria vary by program. Depending on the program, awards may consider academic preparation, professional experience, leadership, and the overall strength of the application.

Scholarship amounts vary by program. Some Brown master's programs offer merit scholarships of up to 50% of tuition.

Programs that offer scholarships up to 50% include Brown's online master's programs as well as the Master's in Cybersecurity program (although limited).

Because tuition and scholarship opportunities differ across programs, prospective students should review their program's specific funding information to understand the potential cost after scholarship assistance.

Brown's cost of attendance is an estimate of the total cost of attending graduate school for a period of time. It can include tuition as well as expenses such as housing, food, health insurance, transportation, books and personal expenses.

Your program's tuition is only one component of that total.

If your master's program requires eight courses, for example, you should look at the [total tuition required for those courses](https://sfs.brown.edu/tuition-and-fees/graduate) rather than assuming that Brown's annual cost-of-attendance figure is the tuition price of your degree.

Brown master's programs have different curricular structures depending on the field and degree. Many traditional on-campus require eight courses, which can be completed over a traditional two-year course of study when students take two courses per semester. Students in the one-year programs complete two courses every quarter.

An eight-course curriculum allows students to pursue a focused master's degree while still having time to engage deeply with their field, develop relationships with faculty and classmates, and take advantage of Brown's academic and professional resources.

The actual time required to complete a degree depends on the program's curriculum, course schedule, enrollment status, and the pace at which a student takes courses. Review your specific program's curriculum and degree requirements for details.

A master's degree can be valuable for many reasons. It can help you advance your career, develop new expertise, change career direction or pursue a subject you're passionate about.

Nationally, workers with master's degrees earn a median 19% more per week than workers with bachelor's degrees, according to the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2025/data-on-display/education-pays.htm).

For some students, earning a master's at Brown is also a personal or lifelong goal—the opportunity to challenge yourself, join an Ivy League academic community or achieve something you've always wanted to do.

The right question is whether the experience and opportunities align with your goals and aspirations.

No. You do not need to have attended an Ivy League institution as an undergraduate to apply to a Brown master's program.

Brown master's students come from a wide range of colleges and universities, academic disciplines and professional backgrounds. Brown has a distinctive culture that many students experience as open, collaborative and welcoming.

Each master's program has its own admissions requirements and evaluates applicants according to its individual criteria.
